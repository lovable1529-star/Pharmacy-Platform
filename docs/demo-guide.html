Karsons Pharmacy Group · Platform walkthrough

Your services, your questions, your data.

A clinical services platform where the questionnaires are yours to build and change — not ours to code. This is what it does, how the parts fit together, and what to look at first.

The shape of it

One loop, from your question to your record

Everything in the platform sits on a single cycle. You describe a service once. Patients answer it. The system checks every answer against your own safety rules the moment it arrives. A pharmacist decides. The record is written, and the surgery is told.

What you learn from running it feeds back into the questions — which is the part most systems make impossible.

Part one

Building a questionnaire

Open Services, pick a service, press Edit form. You get a working surface with three columns: a palette of question types on the left, your form in the middle, and a live preview on the right that behaves exactly as the patient's phone will.

There is no code, no formula language, and nothing that needs us. You add a question by clicking the kind of question it is.

Follow-up questions on any answer

This is the pattern you use most, and it works on any answer, not just Yes. Ask “How did you hear about us?”, and hang a different follow-up off Other than off Friend. A follow-up can have its own follow-up.

Patients only ever see the questions that apply to them. Someone with no allergies never sees an allergy box, so a twelve-question form is often six questions long in practice.

Numbering

“Question 7”

Turn on numbering and every question is numbered automatically, so your team and your patients can refer to the same thing.

Gates

Locked steps

A step can stay locked until earlier answers are given — identity before clinical detail, for instance.

Staff only

Asked on the day

Mark a question as the pharmacist's. It never shows to the patient and appears at the appointment instead.

Warnings

Stop messages

Attach a message to a specific answer — information, caution, or a hard stop shown immediately.

Calculated

BMI and age

Work them out from answers already given, rather than asking the patient to do arithmetic.

Consent

Your wording

The statements a patient agrees to are yours to write, and they are stored with the version they agreed to.

Editing a live form is safe

This is the part worth understanding properly, because it is what lets you change things without fear. Publishing does not overwrite a form. It creates the next version, and every questionnaire a patient has already completed stays permanently attached to the version they actually saw.

Part two

Getting it in front of patients

Four ways in, all leading to the same form and the same record. No patient ever creates an account or remembers a password — a health questionnaire that demands registration does not get filled in.

Part three

The check that runs on every answer

The moment a questionnaire is submitted it is measured against your own rules — the ones you would apply yourself, written down once. Every rule is evaluated, and the system records which rules fired, which did not, and which could not be judged because an answer was missing.

A missing answer never quietly passes a safety rule. That is the single most important sentence on this page.

Worth showing on the day

Open a reviewed submission and press Why?. You get the full reasoning: every rule considered, in order, with the answer it looked at and the conclusion it reached. If a decision is ever questioned — by a patient, an inspector, or a colleague — the answer is on the screen rather than in someone's memory.

Part four

The pharmacist's queue

Repeat care holds everything waiting on a decision, worst first — reds above ambers, and within each band the one that has been waiting longest at the top. The order is worked out across the whole queue, not just the page you are looking at, so a red can never hide on page two.

Opening one shows the answers, the triage decision, the reasoning behind it, and the patient's history. From there a pharmacist can approve, ask the patient for more information, or decline with a reason. Every one of those is recorded against their name and the time.

Approve

Proceed to consultation

The submission moves on and the appointment is ready to start. The decision, the person and the moment are all recorded.

Query

Ask for more

Send it back for one more answer without losing anything already given. The patient reopens the same form where they left it.

Part five

Getting the data back out

Your data is yours, in formats other systems accept. Nothing here is locked in a shape only this platform can read.

The nine o'clock email

At 9pm each evening the system gathers that day's consultations and sends one message per surgery, listing that surgery's patients together in a single table.

This is deliberate. Twenty separate emails into a @gov.im inbox is a nuisance that gets ignored; one clear message a day gets read. Anything it cannot route — a patient with no surgery recorded — is set aside and shown to you rather than being dropped.

Re-running it is safe. A consultation already sent is never included twice, so a retry cannot double-notify a practice.

Built in, not bolted on

Four branches, one organisation. Everything is scoped by branch, so Onchan's list is Onchan's list — while an owner can still see across all of them. Nothing about that arrangement is hard-coded: branches, services, rooms and staff are all things you configure.

On the day

A twelve-minute walkthrough

An order that tells the story properly — configuration first, because that is the part that makes this yours rather than ours.

  1. Services → Edit formAdd a question, give it a follow-up on one specific answer, and watch the preview change as you do it.
  2. Publish itPoint out the version number going up, and that nobody's existing answers moved.
  3. PreviewOpen the patient's view. This is exactly what they get on a phone — no login, no app.
  4. Fill it in as a patientAnswer a few questions, then close the tab halfway through on purpose.
  5. AppointmentsThe booking shows as started, not finished. Reopen and the answers are still there.
  6. Submit itThe safety check runs immediately and the outcome appears.
  7. Repeat care → Why?Show the full reasoning behind the decision. This is the moment that usually lands hardest.
  8. Approve, then start the consultationRecord what was supplied and produce the printed record with its real number.
  9. PatientsOpen the patient and show the whole history sitting in one place.
  10. ExportDownload the consultations list as a spreadsheet — the data is his, and it leaves in a format anything can read.
  11. ComplianceEvery step just taken is already in the audit trail, with a name and a time against it.
