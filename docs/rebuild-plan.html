01 · Position

What the partner settled

Services and the form designer stay. Confirmed as working and out of scope. That is a meaningful result: it means the form schema, the versioned form engine, the 21 field types, conditional reveals and the patient-facing runtime are all proven and can be built on rather than replaced.

Everything else is to be reworked against the new specification.

One judgement I owe you before we start

“Rework everything else” should not mean “delete the database and start again.” Several foundations already match the new specification closely — the audit log, role-based permissions, branch scoping, the notification outbox, the patient record. Rebuilding those from scratch would cost weeks and land in the same place.

So this plan sorts every module into keep, extend or build, with the reason stated. If you want something rebuilt anyway, say which and I will — but I would rather you make that call knowing what it costs.

02 · Architecture

The one idea the whole spec turns on

Section 23 of the specification is the important paragraph, and it is aimed squarely at what is wrong with the current build:

“The system should be built around one connected patient/appointment lifecycle, not as a collection of independent forms.”

That is precisely the fault already found and partly patched here: booking, questionnaire, arrival and consultation were four islands with unpopulated link columns, which is why appointments once read “no form yet” however carefully a patient filled one in. The fix so far has been to join them case by case. The specification asks for the spine to be the design instead.

04 · Defects

Wrong in production right now

Three things found while checking this plan against the live database. None is a design question; all three are live.

1 · No repeat request can be auto-approved

On submission the system computes derived values from the form's own answers but never reads the patient's previous weight or previous strength — even though repeat_enrolment stores both. So weightLossPercent and doseStepChange are always null, and four rules are skipped: dose-skip (RED), stable-continue (GREEN), increase-supported (GREEN) and weight-loss-below-target (AMBER).

Both GREEN routes are among them, so everything falls to the AMBER default and a pharmacist reviews one hundred per cent of requests. The “only same or ±1 strength” block the spec marks BLOCK has also never run. Roughly half a day to fix.

2 · A test question is live on the flu form

The published flu questionnaire (v3) contains a Feedback step with a required question: “Was it difficult to fill the form ?”. Every patient completing the form must answer it before they can submit. It is a leftover from trying out the designer.

3 · The flu form does not match the specification

Against §25.4's nine questions, the live form is missing breastfeeding, bleeding disorder / anticoagulants, and current medication with its conditional detail field. It also asks pregnancy unconditionally rather than driven by gender as §25.3 requires.

05 · Data model

What has to be added

29 tables exist. The specification implies roughly fifteen more. Listed by the workstream that needs them, not alphabetically, because the order is the dependency order.

TableForNote
status_history§7.5Entity, from, to, changed by, reason, timestamp. Underpins the whole state machine; nothing else should be built before it.
medicine · medicine_strength§12Master with an ordered strength ladder — the ±1 step rule needs position, not a text label.
consent_record§8A, §25.6Version, full text snapshot, accepted-at, captured-by, privacy version. Written per submission, never edited.
prescription§18A row of its own rather than columns on the consultation: patient, pharmacist, medicine, strength, quantity, directions, status, signature snapshot.
dispensing_signoff · collection_signoff§8.3, §8.4Who dispensed, who collected, when, signature.
document§10Register with a category, linked to patient and appointment, pointing at private storage.
vaccine_administration§27, §29.5With snapshot columns for vaccine name, batch, expiry, pharmacist name and registration number.
gp_notification§8.6Per-event log with delivery status and result, replacing the single column on the consultation.
urgent_task§6.3The RED queue, with follow-up call recorded.
notification_template§15Editable templates rather than strings in code.
inventory_category§9The category layer above the existing product table.
pharmacist_declaration§26.4Each declaration stored as its own timestamped confirmation against the consultation.
The snapshot rule, applied everywhere

§29.5 states it for vaccination and it should be the rule for the whole system: when a record completes, copy the master data into it — vaccine name, batch, expiry, pharmacist name, registration number, medicine, strength, price, consent wording. Editing a master record next year must never change what a historical record says happened.

The form engine already works this way, binding a submission to the version it was answered against. Extending the same principle to master data is the single most valuable structural decision in this rebuild.

06 · Workstreams

Nine pieces of work

A

Repair what is live

~1 day

The three defects above, done first because they are cheap and two of them are visible to patients today.

  • Feed previous weight and strength into the derived values.
  • Remove the feedback step from the flu form and republish.
  • Re-evaluate historical submissions so later comparisons have a true baseline.
B

The lifecycle spine

~5–7 days

Status history, the state machine, and record locking. Everything else depends on this, so it goes in before any screen is rebuilt.

  • status_history, written by every transition rather than by callers who remember to.
  • Draft → Pending → Approved / Rejected → Resubmitted, enforced server-side; illegal transitions refused.
  • Mark-as-pending as a deliberate pharmacist action (§7.2), not only an initial state.
  • Rejection requires reason, pharmacist, GPhC number and signature.
  • Approved questionnaires lock; corrections create a revision and an audit event.
C

Masters and configuration

~4–5 days

Everything the spec says must be configurable rather than coded — including its own note that unconfirmed clinical values belong in the admin panel, not in the source.

  • Medicine master with an ordered strength ladder; GP practice master; pharmacist master with registration numbers.
  • Vaccine products and batches with expiry, quantity and branch.
  • Slot configuration: working days, breaks, closed dates, max per slot.
  • Seed from the lists supplied in the spec, all editable afterwards.
D

GLP-1 repeat care, end to end

~8–10 days

The largest clinical workstream, and the one with the most detail already specified.

  • Enrolment gate: Repeat Care ID + email must match an active record before the form opens (§4.2).
  • Repeat request form rebuilt to §4.3, with dose options constrained to ±1 step from the last actual supply.
  • System Recommendation shown read-only to the patient before their own request (§4.3).
  • Holiday supply rules (§5.5) — entirely new.
  • Separated dashboard queues and the urgent RED task list (§6.1, §6.3).
  • Internal pharmacy repeat form so a counter supply still writes history (§6.5).
E

Flu vaccination, end to end

~6–8 days

Built as a reusable vaccination engine, because §28.2 is explicit that COVID, hepatitis, shingles and the rest follow through the same machinery.

  • Questionnaire corrected to §25.4, with the gender-driven pregnancy and breastfeeding logic.
  • Pharmacist search-first home: find by name or date of birth, open or start (§26.1).
  • Administration record to §27, including the site-of-administration rule that hides injection type for oral, nasal and topical.
  • Pharmacist declarations stored as timestamped confirmations.
  • Batch deduction on completion, expired batches unselectable, stock never negative.
F

Prescription and dispensing

~5–6 days

The prescription becomes a record with a lifecycle rather than a generated file.

  • Prescription table, statuses, and generation gated on the payment condition being satisfied.
  • Front and back content to §8.2, including price and paid / pay-on-collection.
  • Dispensing sign-off, collection sign-off, branch routing.
  • Patient questions flagged prominently at dispensing (§6.4).
G

Documents, calendar, dashboard

~5–6 days
  • Document register with categories, linked to patient and appointment, private storage throughout.
  • Month / week / day calendar with status colouring and click-through.
  • Dashboard counts and quick appointment.
H

Notifications and reporting

~4–5 days
  • Templates as editable records; the six trigger points in §15.
  • SMS kept free of clinical detail, as the spec requires.
  • The ten reports in §21, aggregated in the database rather than truncated in the client.
I

Configurable rules

~8–10 days

Already planned in detail separately. The spec reinforces it — “these should be configurable in the admin panel rather than hard-coded” — so it moves from a nice-to-have to a stated requirement.

Total

Roughly 46 to 57 working days of focused work, or about nine to twelve weeks single-handed. Workstream A is one day and should go in this week regardless of what else is agreed.

07 · Sequence

Build order, and why

The spec's own order (§22) starts with authentication and roles, which already exist here. Reordered for this codebase and for dependency:

#WorkstreamWhy here
1A · Repair what is liveOne day; two defects are patient-facing today.
2B · Lifecycle spineStatus history and locking must exist before any screen writes a status, or every screen gets rewritten twice.
3C · MastersThe medicine strength ladder is a hard dependency of the GLP-1 dose rules; vaccine batches are a hard dependency of flu.
4E · Flu vaccinationBefore GLP-1 deliberately: smaller, fully specified, no payment coupling, and its season starts sooner. It proves the spine end to end on the easier service.
5D · GLP-1 repeat careThe largest and most conditional; benefits from the spine being proven first.
6F · Prescription & dispensingNeeds D's decision outcomes and C's medicine master.
7G · Documents, calendar, dashboardPresentation over data that by now exists.
8H · Notifications & reportingReports are only meaningful once the records they read are correct.
9I · Configurable rulesLast, and best done once there is real data to replay a draft ruleset against.
The one sequencing call worth challenging

Flu before GLP-1 is a deliberate inversion of the emphasis in the specification. Flu is smaller, has no payment or prescription coupling, and its questionnaire is fully written out — so it is the cheapest way to prove the spine works before committing the larger workstream to it. If the client's priority is GLP-1, say so and I will swap them, accepting that the spine gets proven on the harder service.

08 · Blocking questions

What I need answered

QuestionWhy it blocks
Weeks on current dose — patient's answer, or our supply record? Nearly every dose and supply rule depends on it. repeat_enrolment.strength_since already exists, and the record is not gameable. The prior scope of work worries explicitly about patients circumventing checks.
Does Repeat Care ID stay the Pharmadoctor ID? The current field is external_ref. If they are moving off Pharmadoctor, we should issue our own identifier now rather than migrate later.
Payment — still simulated, or real for this build? §8.1 makes prescription generation conditional on payment. Simulation is fine for the workflow, but the condition has to be honest about which mode it is in.
Do appointments and consultations merge? The spec treats “appointment” as the central record carrying clinical status. We have both. Keeping two risks re-creating the island problem the spec exists to solve.
Which flu clinical rules, if any, are automatic? §25.4 is explicit: do not invent eligibility logic beyond what the pharmacy approves. So flu ships with pharmacist judgement unless Muka's clinical lead signs off specific rules.
Confirm the four roles map onto ours. Spec lists Patient, Admin, Pharmacist, Reception. Patients currently have no account at all — forms are anonymous by design. §16.1 assumes patients log in to view their own status.
The biggest of these

The patient-account question changes the shape of the build. Today a questionnaire needs no login, which was a deliberate decision — a health form that demands registration does not get filled in. But §16.1 requires patients to view their own status and permitted edits, which needs accounts, ownership checks and password reset. That is a workstream in itself and it is not costed above. I need a decision before B starts.

09 · Boundaries

What this plan does not include

  • Services and the form designer — untouched by instruction, beyond correcting the flu questionnaire's content through the designer itself.
  • Real payment processing — the workflow and conditions are built; the gateway waits on the NMS and GPhC position.
  • Front CMS (§2) — listed as a module with no requirements given anywhere in the document.
  • Patient accounts — excluded until the question above is answered, then costed separately.
  • Database region move — unrelated to this spec, but still the largest single speed improvement available and worth doing during a rebuild rather than after.

Two clinical caveats the specification states and this plan inherits: final eligibility rules, PGDs and mandatory-field decisions must be confirmed by the pharmacy's clinical lead before production; and no automatic flu eligibility logic will be invented beyond what they approve.
