Ten questions only you can answer

Every one of these is a clinical or commercial decision rather than a technical one, which is why we have not guessed. Each says what we have assumed in the meantime and what it costs to leave it open — so you can answer the four that matter and leave the rest.

Blocking

These four are stopping work. Everything else can proceed around them.

01

What does it cost?

blocking

The question

What does a new patient pay, and what does a repeat supply cost? Does the price change with the medicine, the strength, or how many months they take?

What happens now

Neither service has a price, so there is no payment step at all — the prescription is simply released. Your staff are never asked to confirm anything, and nobody is ever asked for money.

Until we have prices, the service gives medicine away. It is the single largest gap between what is built and what you can actually open.

02

Should repeat care switch itself on?

blocking

The question

At the moment, as soon as a new patient's first supply actually leaves the pharmacy, they are enrolled in repeat care automatically and given a Repeat Care ID. From then on they can order online without speaking to anybody.

Is that what you want — or should a pharmacist have to switch it on deliberately for each patient?

What we built, and why

Automatic on first supply. Your own workflow says a patient's history should only move forward after a real supply, and without something doing this a remote patient could complete the entire journey and still never be able to order a repeat — the pathway simply dead-ended.

This is a clinical authorisation happening without a human pressing anything. We are comfortable with the reasoning but it is your decision, not ours. If you would rather it were deliberate, it is a small change — but it needs deciding before patients are using it.

03

What happens to somebody who does not meet the BMI rule?

blocking

The question

You told us: BMI 30 and above, or 27 and above with a weight-related condition. Somebody with a BMI of 28 and no qualifying condition falls outside that.

Should the form stop them there and explain why, or let them submit so a pharmacist can look at the whole picture?

What happens now

They can submit, and your pharmacist sees a clear note saying they do not meet the criterion and why. Nobody is ever silently passed as eligible — but nobody is turned away either.

These are genuinely different products. Stopping them is cleaner and cheaper to run; letting them through catches the patient who should have been seen but whose numbers happen to fall short. We did not want to choose that on your behalf.

04

What do we ask a patient transferring from another clinic?

blocking

The question

You gave us the route and the categories — prior clinic, baseline, treatment evidence — and the rule that a current BMI between 20 and 25 may only proceed as a verified continuation.

We need the actual questions, and what counts as acceptable proof. A photograph of the box? A prescription? A letter from the clinic? Does a screenshot of an app count?

What happens now

The branch exists and asks the categories you named: which clinic, which medicine and strength, when that strength started, when they last had a supply, what they weighed when treatment began, and any side effects. It also asks for a photograph of their current prescription or medicine box.

We have deliberately not invented clinical wording to fill the rest.

Somebody transferring is arguably your highest-risk patient — already on a medicine, and you have only their word for what and how much. The questions matter more here than anywhere else on the form.

Needed soon

Not blocking today, but they shape work already scheduled.

05

When is a three-month supply allowed?

needed soon

The question

Your workflow allows one, two or three months "where permitted" without saying when. Is it about how long they have been stable, which medicine, which strength, or a holiday?

What happens now

A three-month request is flagged for a pharmacist to review. Not refused, not waved through.

Inventing a permission would be as wrong as inventing a restriction, so it stays under review until you tell us. It simply means more work for your team than it needs to be.

06

What is the clinic called?

needed soon

The question

You said you want this marketed as a separate business and that nobody should know it is a Karsons product. We need the name, a logo, the colours, a support email and telephone number, and the link to your face-to-face programme for patients who want to be seen.

We also need the terms and conditions and privacy wording you want patients to agree to.

What happens now

Patient-facing pages carry Karsons branding. The system is built to swap that for a separate identity while keeping Karsons named as the dispensing pharmacy — which is a legal requirement and not something we would hide.

Nothing is broken, but you cannot launch it as a separate clinic until this arrives.

07

Which information leaflets, and which are compulsory?

needed soon

The question

You asked for treatment, risk and side-effect material shown as links the patient ticks to confirm they have read before signing. We need the actual links — and which of them a patient must tick, as opposed to being offered.

Do they differ between Mounjaro and Wegovy?

What happens now

The mechanism is built, including recording exactly which version of which leaflet a patient agreed to — so if you update a link next year, last year's record still says what they actually saw. There is simply no content in it.

You described this as part of how the service is safe without seeing anybody. At the moment that part is missing.

Who is allowed to do what?

can wait

The question

Can any trained member of staff make the verification call, or must it be a pharmacist? And separately — who may press approve? We can restrict that to registered prescribers only.

Also: how do you want identity confirmed on the telephone? Date of birth and address, or something else? Whatever you say, the system will record that the check was done.

Anybody with review access can record a call and approve. The system records who did each, and captures how they say they confirmed identity — but does not prescribe the method.

can wait

The question

How many attempts before a request is parked or closed? Should the patient be emailed asking them to ring back? Should it drop off the queue after a fortnight?
