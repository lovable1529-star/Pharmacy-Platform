Karsons Pharmacy Platform · Operating workflow

How the system works now

The two clinical journeys end to end, what happens at each step and where, and the six scripts to run before any of it is live.

The idea

One patient lifecycle, two clinical routes

The rebuild turns on a single principle from the specification: one connected patient lifecycle rather than a collection of independent forms. Everything hangs off that spine, and the two services differ only in the middle of it.

Journey one

GLP-1 repeat care

From a pharmacist authorising somebody onto repeat care, to the medicine leaving the counter.

  1. Authorise the patientStaff After an initial consultation and a first follow-up, a pharmacist enrols them. Patients cannot enrol themselves — the gate exists so a repeat request is never the first time anyone has looked at someone. /patients/[id]
  2. Patient starts a requestPatient Repeat Care ID and the email we hold. Both must match an active enrolment. No account, no password. Every failure gives the same message and a booking link — telling somebody which half they got right would turn the gate into a tool for finding valid IDs. /repeat/weight-management-repeat
  3. The questionnaire, tailored to themPatient The dose dropdown offers their current strength or one step either way — nothing else is selectable. A read-only recommendation appears before they choose, so they are deciding against advice rather than in a vacuum. Answers save as they type. /f/weight-management-repeat
  4. Triage, the moment they submitSystem Every rule is evaluated against their answers and their previous supply. Consent is snapshotted with the exact wording shown. A missing answer never quietly satisfies a safety rule. GREEN → approved · AMBER → queue · RED → urgent
  5. Pharmacist decidesStaff Separate queues for red, amber, green and “asked a question”. Reds also raise an urgent task in its own list, because “look at this today” and “ring this patient now” are different jobs. Why? shows every rule considered and the answer it read. /repeat-care
  6. Approval raises a prescriptionSystem Raised, not issued: no number and no document until the payment condition is met. Allocating a number for a request never paid for would leave a gap in the sequence that reads later like a missing prescription. status: awaiting payment
  7. PaymentPatient Online, or on collection. Settling issues the prescription, allocates its per-branch number, and queues a “ready to collect” message in wording the pharmacy controls. /pay/[token]
  8. DispenseStaff The dispensing pharmacist signs off. If the patient asked a question, it is shown on the row and again in the dialog, and sign-off is blocked until they confirm they raised it. /prescriptions
  9. CollectionStaff Whoever collects prints their name and it is recorded — noting whether that was the patient or somebody else. Collection cannot be recorded before dispensing. /prescriptions
If they come in instead

A repeat care patient who sees the pharmacist before supply is handled at /repeat-care/new. It opens the patient’s own questionnaire, so the triage rules, dose limits and consent wording are identical — and the repeat care history gets its supply entry either way. Without that, the next request would measure weight loss against a supply that was never recorded.

Journey two

Vaccination

Built as a general vaccination engine rather than a flu one — COVID, shingles, hepatitis and the rest run through the same machinery. Adding one is configuration, not development.

  1. Book, or walk inPatient Online, by QR code, or at the counter. Slots respect opening hours, lunch breaks and closed days, so nobody can book a time the pharmacy is shut. /book · /appointments/new
  2. Fill in the questionnairePatient Before arriving, or on a tablet in the shop. Pregnancy and breastfeeding appear only where they apply. Fever in the last 24 hours is deliberately not asked in advance — it is a question about the day itself. /f/flu-vaccination
  3. Find themStaff The pharmacist starts by searching a name or date of birth, not by navigating a table. Already-vaccinated patients stay in the results, because “did she have hers?” is a question this screen has to answer. /vaccinations
  4. Read the answers backStaff Anything worth a second look is marked. Consent is checked from the record rather than assumed. /vaccinations/[id]
  5. Record what was givenStaff Branch, pharmacist, vaccine and batch, site and route. Choosing a pharmacist fills in their registration number; choosing a batch fills in its expiry. Only in-date, un-recalled, in-stock batches are offered. Oral, nasal and topical routes hide the injection type entirely. /vaccinations/[id]
  6. Confirm the declarationsStaff Four statements, each stored as its own timestamped confirmation with the wording as it stood. “Confirmed four things” and “ticked a box” are different claims. /vaccinations/[id]
  7. CompleteSystem One transaction: the record with its snapshots, the declarations, the stock coming out of that branch, the GP notification queued, the document filed, the questionnaire locked as clinical history. None of those is true without the others. stock decremented · GP queued
Every evening

At 9pm each GP surgery receives one email listing that day’s patients together, rather than twenty separate messages. Anything that cannot be routed — a patient with no surgery recorded — is set aside and shown to you rather than dropped.

Before it is live

The six scripts, in order

Run them in the Supabase SQL editor, or with psql. Order matters — 18 and 19 reference tables that 17 creates. Each ends with a verification query that should come back with counts rather than zeros.

-- 1. The corrected flu questionnaire. RUN ONCE ONLY.
platform/supabase/15_flu_form_v4.sql

-- 2. Status history and the two states the state machine needed
platform/supabase/16_status_history.sql

-- 3. Medicine master, GP master, breaks and closures
platform/supabase/17_masters.sql

-- 4. Vaccination records and pharmacist declarations
platform/supabase/18_vaccination.sql

-- 5. Prescriptions, sign-offs, documents, consent, GP log
platform/supabase/19_prescription_documents.sql

-- 6. Inventory transaction types and message templates
platform/supabase/20_inventory_notifications.sql
One of them is not repeatable

15_flu_form_v4.sql publishes a new version of the flu questionnaire. Running it twice publishes two. The other five are safe to re-run.

Then, before anyone uses it

Push, and set NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL in Vercel if it is still unset — and redeploy, because Next inlines it at build time. Without it every booking, payment and invitation link points at localhost, and nothing errors to tell you.

Deliberately off

What is not switched on

ThingState, and why
Real payments The workflow, conditions and statuses are built and work. No money moves and no card details are handled — the gateway waits on the NMS and GPhC position.
The four nightly jobs Scheduled in vercel.json and guarded by CRON_SECRET, which fails closed. With no secret set they return 401 and do nothing — correct, since those endpoints are reachable from the public internet.
Email and SMS delivery Queued and logged rather than sent. Email needs SPF, DKIM and DMARC aligned before @gov.im addresses will accept anything.
Patient accounts Not built, by your decision. Forms stay anonymous, gated by token or ID and email.
Automatic flu eligibility rules None invented. The specification is explicit that no eligibility logic should exist beyond what the pharmacy approves, so a pharmacist judges suitability against the PGD.
Still worth an hour

The database is in Seoul. Every page makes about seven round trips to it, which is most of what a page costs. Moving the project to a London region takes a typical screen from roughly two seconds to about three hundred milliseconds, and is the right home for Isle of Man patient data regardless.
