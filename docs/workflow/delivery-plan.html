Do this before anything else

The migration ledger does not exist yet

The _migration table is absent — not empty, absent. --baseline records whatever SQL files are sitting in the folder at that moment and runs none of them. So the order is not negotiable:

# 1. While ONLY scripts 01–20 are in platform/supabase/
pnpm db:migrate --baseline

# 2. Now copy 21 and 22 in, then
pnpm db:migrate

Do it the other way round — scripts 21 and 22 copied in first, then baseline — and both are marked applied without ever running. You would have a ledger claiming success over a database with none of the new tables, and the first symptom would be runtime errors pointing nowhere near the cause. The implementation package's README does not know about the baseline mechanism, so it cannot warn you.

06

Fulfilment — and the gap that breaks the chain

2–3 daysneeds 05plan gap

The supplied plan has a dead end here. Repeat access requires a repeat_enrolment row, and no phase in the package ever creates one. Under the new model a NEW patient has no consultation to enrol from — so as written, a remote patient completes the entire journey and can then never use the repeat service.

  • Create the enrolment on first SUPPLIED, with the baseline taken from the prescriber-authorised values. This follows the plan's own rule that history moves only after a real supply, and it closes the dead end.
  • Create one prescription_fulfilment per issued prescription, from the patient's collection-or-delivery choice, snapshotting the delivery address.
  • Batch number and expiry are mandatory before READY, DISPATCHED, COLLECTED or SUPPLIED, and expiry must be later than the supply date. Enforced in the database by migration 21 and again server-side so the error is legible.
  • Collection reuses collection_signoff; delivery records carrier, tracking, dispatch time and staff member. Reuse dispensing_signoff rather than adding a second signature table.
  • Repeat baseline updates only after supply, using supplied values rather than requested ones.

Blocked on the client — do not invent these

Each has a configurable slot to build now and content to fill in later. Writing clinical wording ourselves is the one thing this project cannot afford.

Transfer patient questions

The route, the evidence categories and the BMI 20–<25 rule are supplied. The individual prior-clinic, baseline and treatment-evidence questions are not, nor is what counts as acceptable proof.

Three-month supply

The workflow image says "where permitted" without saying when. Stays AMBER until Muka defines it — by product, dose, stability or holiday reason.

Standalone clinic identity

Brand name, logo, colours, support email and phone, terms, privacy text, and the F2F referral URL. He is explicit that nobody should know it is a Karsons product.

Digital resources

The actual treatment, risk and side-effect links, and which are mandatory for which medicine. He asked for these as clickable links with a tick to confirm they were read.

Dispatch

Whether carrier and tracking should be automated at all, and with whom. Manual entry is assumed until told otherwise.

Enrolment trigger

We propose enrolling a patient into repeat care automatically on their first supply. It closes the dead end in Stage 06, but it is a clinical authorisation and should be confirmed rather than assumed.

What could go wrong

Baselining in the wrong order highest

Silent, and it looks like success. Covered at the top of this document; it is the only item here that can destroy work rather than delay it.

Publishing over form version 1 medium

Two submissions were answered against v1. Editing it in place would rewrite what those patients were asked, which is exactly what the versioning exists to prevent. Stage 03 publishes v2 and leaves v1 untouched.

The chain still untested in reality medium

Zero rule evaluations exist. Every clinical rule is proven by unit tests and none by a real submission. Enrolling one test patient and putting a request through is the cheapest way to find out what the tests do not cover — and it should happen before Stage 07, not after.

Season versus scope medium

Twenty to twenty-five focused days against a September flu season. Stage 02 protects flu early and cheaply; the weight-management work is what needs the runway. If something has to give, it is stages 08 to 10, and that should be said out loud now rather than discovered in three weeks.
