Section 01What the platform is

A single system for running Karsons' clinical services end to end: the patient fills in a questionnaire online, the answers are triaged automatically, a pharmacist makes the decision, and the platform carries it through to a prescription, a payment, a dispatch and a note to the patient's GP.

It replaces paper and spreadsheets for three things at once — capturing the clinical information, deciding what is safe, and recording what happened and why. The third is the part that matters most under inspection, and it is why nothing clinical in this system can be deleted or quietly edited.

Two branches

Onchan (ONC) and Kirk Michael (KMI). Staff are granted access per branch and per date range, so a locum can hold Pharmacist at one branch for a fortnight.

Five roles

Admin, Pharmacist, Technician, Reception, Viewer. Permissions are a grid of module × action, editable by an administrator without a deployment.

Remote by default

Weight management is delivered online: the patient never routinely comes in. Flu vaccination is the opposite — booked and given in person.

Section 02The three services

Everything the platform does is driven by a service. A service owns a questionnaire, optionally a rulebook, a price, its leaflets and its public branding. Adding a fourth service is configuration, not development.

Why versions matter

A published questionnaire is never edited. Changing it publishes a new version, and every submission stays bound to the version it was answered against. So a request from March still shows the questions that patient was actually asked, even after the form has moved on twice. The same is true of the rulebook.

Section 03Journey A — a new weight-management patient

Ten steps, about ten minutes, entirely online. The patient has no account and never creates one.

1

Patient

Chooses the pathway

The form opens by explaining that this is an online service. If they would rather be seen in person it stops there and offers them the face-to-face programme instead — a button, not just a sentence. Nothing further is asked.

2

Patient

Answers 52 questions across nine more steps

Identity and contact, measurements, medical history, habits and safety, what they are asking for, evidence uploads, how they want it delivered, and consent. Answers save as they type, so they can close the page and come back.

3

Patient

Uploads photo ID, a photo of themselves and their scales

Three required attachments. A transfer patient from another clinic also uploads their current prescription or medicine box.

4

Patient

Reads the leaflets, signs, submits

Anything the pharmacy has marked as required must be ticked before the step will advance. They are then given a link to track the request.

5

Pharmacist

Reads it, and telephones the patient

The request appears on Weight Management → New patients with a badge showing how many are still to call. The call is recorded against the request: who spoke to them, when, whether identity was verified, and what was established.

6

Pharmacist

Approves, defers, declines or escalates

Approval is refused by the server until a completed, identity-verified call exists. This is a gate, not a disabled button.

7

System

Prescription, payment, GP note, enrolment

A numbered prescription is raised, a payment is created at the service price, the patient's GP is notified, and the patient is enrolled in repeat care with a Repeat Care ID of the form RC-XXXX-XXXX.

8

Technician

Assembles, batches and sends

Fulfilment moves through pending → assembling → ready → dispatched or collected. A batch number and expiry are required before it can reach ready, enforced by the database rather than the screen.

Section 04Journey B — a repeat request

Shorter, and the one the automatic triage was built for. The patient must already be enrolled: they cannot enrol themselves.

1

Patient

Enters their Repeat Care ID and email

Both must match an active enrolment. A failure gives one message whatever went wrong — telling a caller which half they got right would turn this into a tool for finding valid IDs.

2

Patient

Answers six steps about how they are getting on

The form already knows their medicine and current strength, so the dose options are personalised to them rather than being a free choice.

3

System

All 24 rules run, and a colour comes out

Explained in the next section. Every rule that ran is recorded, including the ones that did not fire and why.

4

Pharmacist

Authorises — with different amounts of work per colour

Green a compact authorisation, no phone call needed. Amber the pharmacist must record why they are content. Red cannot be supplied from this screen at all.

5

System

Same tail as a new patient

Prescription, payment, dispatch — and the enrolment's last-supplied date moves, which is what the due list reads.

Section 05How green, amber and red are decided

Every rule runs on every request. There is no early exit, and the order rules are written in does not change the answer.

Red wins

If any red rule matched, the request is red — whatever else also matched.

Otherwise amber

If no red matched but an amber did, the request is amber.

Otherwise green

Only when nothing more severe matched at all.

Nothing matched? Amber

Silence is not treated as evidence of safety. The default is deliberately cautious.

A rule whose question the patient did not answer is recorded as skipped — never as passed. That distinction is the whole safety model: a missing answer can never earn a green.

The 24 repeat rules, in short

OutcomeCountWhat they look for
Red7 Pregnancy or breastfeeding · two or more missed doses · severe or red-flag side effects · a dose jump skipping a strength · BMI under 23 while not reducing · Wegovy outside 18–74 · Mounjaro outside 18–84
Amber11 One missed dose · moderate side effects · medicines or conditions changed · weight loss below the 2% monthly target · appetite suppression wearing off or frequent snacking · a dose change before three weeks · two pens without six weeks stable · more than two months requested · BMI 23–24.9 · the patient asked a question · the patient asked to come in
Green6 Stable on the same dose and meeting target · an increase supported by the answers and eligible on timing · BMI under 23 with a taper request · mild side effects · good fluid intake · low fluid intake
The new-patient rulebook is different on purpose

It has 6 rules, not 24 — the eligibility ones only. The other 18 ask about treatment so far: side effects, doses missed, weeks on the current strength, weight lost since starting. A patient who has not started has no answer to any of them, so copying those across would have put eighteen rules on screen that could never fire.

Section 06What a colour is not allowed to do

A rules engine can say a request looks routine. It cannot prescribe. Several checks sit between a colour and a supply, and each one is enforced on the server rather than by hiding a button.

GateRefuses when
New-patient approvalNo completed, identity-verified telephone call is recorded against the request.
Repeat authorisationThe outcome is red; or the patient is not actively enrolled; or an amber is being approved with no reasoning written down; or nothing triaged it and nothing was recorded.
Measurement sanityThe height or weight could not have come from a person, so the BMI and weight-change checks never ran. Blocked whatever colour the engine reached, and a note does not clear it.
FulfilmentA batch number and expiry are missing, or the expiry is not later than the supply date. Enforced by a database trigger.
DeletionAlways, for clinical records. Patients, submissions, evaluations and acknowledgements cannot be deleted — only archived or superseded.

Section 08What the pharmacy can change itself

The point of the design: almost everything a pharmacy wants to change month to month is configuration, reachable from Services, with no developer involved.

Edit form

The designer. 21 question types, conditional follow-ups, step gating, warnings. Publishing creates a new version and leaves everything already answered untouched.

Links and leaflets

What a patient must read, where it appears on the form, and whether they must tick to confirm. Edits are live immediately — no republish.

Clinical rules

Read every rule as a sentence, and tune outcomes, priorities, thresholds and messages. Publishing mints a new rules version; the old one stays.

Settings

Price, and the public branding — clinic name, colours, support email and phone, privacy and terms links.

A check worth knowing about

The rules screen warns if a rule reads a question the current form no longer asks. Renaming a field in the designer would otherwise break that rule silently — it would simply stop matching, and the request would come back amber looking like an ordinary cautious result.

Section 09Safety and record-keeping

  • Nothing clinical is deleted. A database trigger refuses it on twelve tables. Records are archived, closed or superseded.
  • Consent is snapshotted. The exact wording the patient was shown is copied into the record, so a later rewording cannot change what they agreed to.
  • Leaflet acknowledgements are snapshotted too — the title, the link and the version as they stood that day.
  • The audit log is hash-chained. Each entry carries the hash of the one before it, so a removed or altered entry is detectable.
  • Text messages never carry clinical detail. A template not permitted it never receives it, whatever the caller passes in — including leaflet links, because a link title names the medicine.
  • Implausible measurements are refused. A height typed in metres cannot produce a BMI, and cannot be authorised on.

Section 11What is deliberately not built yet

Listed so nobody reports these as faults.

  • No card payments. Payment is a "received" tickbox, at the client's request. Nothing takes money.
  • Rules can be tuned but not added or removed. Thresholds, outcomes, priorities and messages are editable; creating a brand-new rule or changing the shape of a condition is not.
  • Branding is placeholder. The clinic name, colours and support details are stand-ins until the client supplies real ones.
  • The face-to-face page is a placeholder and says so on itself. It is replaced by pointing the referral link at the pharmacy's own page.
  • No paging on long lists. Fine at present volumes; owed before the lists get long.
  • Demo patients are permanent. Clinical records cannot be deleted, so the five above can be archived and closed but never removed.
